LEI-V3 wing corrections: canopy billowing and the short structural chord#

Two corrections come up whenever an aerodynamic mesh is built on the LEI-V3 wireframe. V3Kite.jl ships both of them (billowing_percentage: 5, aero_scale_chord: 0.129), and they are easy to dismiss as tuning factors. They are not. Each one has a measured or geometric origin, and only one of them is specific to this kite.

1. Canopy billowing (5 %)#

What is measured. Haanen (MSc thesis, TU Delft, 2026, Sec. 9.2.1) reconstructs the flying V3 from photogrammetry and measures the distance between the trailing-edge markers of consecutive struts. Averaged over the span, relative to the CAD:

The leading edge spacing barely changes (the inflated LE tube holds it). The canopy between struts bulges into a spanwise arc, so the straight TE chord between two struts shortens while the fabric itself does not stretch. Billowing grows with load, but not linearly.

What the 5 % means in VSM.jl. billowing_percentage = p puts the TE arc at 1/(1 - p/100) times the straight strut-to-strut edge, i.e. the straight edge is (1 - p/100) of the fabric length. With p = 5 that is Haanen’s powered -5 %. VSM.jl then gives the arc the shape -A sin(pi t) (mid-bay chords rotated nose-down about the rib-to-rib LE axis).

Shape: the circular arc. Cayon (MSc thesis, TU Delft/DTU, 2022, Sec. 5.3) derives the spanwise shape. A tensioned, inextensible membrane under a locally constant pressure jump Delta p has constant tension f = Delta p R. The shape is therefore a circular arc, and its radius does not depend on the magnitude of Delta p, because scaling Delta p by K scales the tension by K too. The billowing then changes only through the TE length between struts: a shorter straight TE with the same fabric length gives a deeper arc. That thesis took the arc angles from the CAD (Table A.3: 1, 10, 15, 18, 20 deg from the tip to the centre plate). The V3’s LE frame is designed with the orientation of the expected powered billow (B. van Ostheim, Kitepower).

Billow full fidelity resolves it. struc_geometry_FEM_full.yaml carries TE canopy nodes between the struts, so the membrane solve shows the billowing directly. Measured on the stored results (strut TE nodes 2, 8, 14, …, 56; inner seven bays averaged; date 2026-10-28):

The TE fabric polyline stays within 0.5 % of its built length, so the inextensible-canopy assumption holds. The level (-5 to -8 %) and the trend with load agree with the photogrammetry. One difference: Billow contracts the outermost bays most (-9 to -15 %), whereas Haanen sees the smallest change there. The cause is not identified yet; it may be the tip loading in the simulation or the reconstruction uncertainty at the tips.

Caveat: the Billow full coupling is not final. Its leading-edge aerodynamic coupling still lacks proper handling of the LE shape and of the moment transfer, so the loads behind these numbers are not yet right. Read the table as “Billow full reproduces the mechanism and the order of magnitude”, not as a validated billowing law.

Consequence. Billowing is physics, not tuning. On a mesh that has no canopy between the struts (the wireframe), it has to be added to the aerodynamic mesh. Billow full computes it, within the caveat above.

2. The short structural chord (x1.129, LEI-V3 only)#

The PSM wireframe (struc_geometry.yaml, struc_geometry_PSM_reduced_photogrammetry_adjusted.yaml, removed on 2026-10-29 and kept in v0.1.0) puts the wing nodes at the bridle attachment points. The TE node is the strut’s rear bridle point, not the end of the fabric (x/c about 0.01 to 0.90). An aerodynamic mesh built on those nodes (LinearStructuralToAeroMapper) flies a wing that is too short in chord:

19.41 / 17.21 = 1.128: this is V3Kite’s 1.129 (“12.9 % was the delta in projected area”, V3Kite.jl data/struc_geometry.yaml). system.yaml’s projected_surface_area: 17.2119 is the area of the PSM nodes, not of the real wing.

Where AWETrim stands.

  • Billow full and wireframe.driver.solve_deformation_reduced_fem fly the full-chord edges (SkinStructuralToAeroMapper carries the FEM edges on the reduced ribs). They need no factor.

  • LinearStructuralToAeroMapper on the PSM wireframe flies the short wing. The shipped rom_config_aerostructural.yaml was identified on this one (identification.yaml, fidelity wireframe). Its coefficients are normalised by 19.75 m2 but come from a 17.2 m2 wing.

  • The geometric fix (put the TE at the end of the fabric) is better than the force factor. A uniform x1.129 on force and moment keeps CL/CD at equal lift and leaves the pitching-moment arm short.

A factor like this belongs to a kite’s data folder, never to code or a generic default: another mesh has another deficit, or none.

3. What AWETrim does (since 2026-10-28)#

The official identification (identification.yaml, fidelity wireframe) flies the wireframe reduced from the full FEM file (wireframe.driver.build_case_reduced_fem): one structural file for both fidelities, the full-chord aerodynamic mesh (no area factor), and:

  • Billowing (aerodynamic.billowing.contraction: 0.05, aerostructural/billowing.py). Every strut rotates about its leading-edge node in the wing surface until each inner bay’s straight trailing edge is (1 - p) of the CAD one; the canopy between two struts is the circular arc of the bay’s built fabric length (sin(theta)/theta = c/L_f), each section at its own fabric fraction. The two tip bays cannot contract by rotating the 0.35 m tip chord; they ride rigidly with the outer strut (Haanen also sees the least change there). The reduced ribs move with their struts and the wing members rest at the new distances, so the structure holds the contraction the aero mesh is drawn with; the bridle keeps its lengths. The arc is built once into the reference mesh and carried by the skin mapper (static billowing). At p = 5 % the inner bays read 1 - c/L_f = 6.1-8.3 %, theta 35-41 deg, depth 198-235 mm, and the outer strut rotates 9 deg.

  • Exact load transfer (aerodynamic.load_transfer: strut_lever), see strut_lever_load_transfer.md.

The billowed wing needs about twice the resolution of the flat one (the arc must be resolved). 16 sections (432 panels) agree with 12 to 0.03 %; at 20 (540 panels) VSM’s circulation loop no longer converges within its iteration limit. At 12 deg the values move +-1 % without trend from 3 sections on (panels near stall changing branch with the mesh), as in the trim panel-count study. The quarter-chord force directions (is_aoa_corrected, off by default) give the same mesh convergence but change the values: CD -11 to -16 %, CMy -0.04 to -0.08, CL < 0.3 %. Default: 5 sections, 135 panels.

At fixed angle of attack, 5 % billowing lowers CL by about 20 % (0.82 to 0.66 at 8 deg): the arc pitches the mid-bay sections nose down by about 3 deg on average. A trim recovers most of it in angle of attack; at equal lift L/D drops roughly 5 % (interpolated, not yet from a trim).

History of the confusion#

On 2026-10-27/28 both factors were ported as “V3Kite emulation” options on the local branch exp/v3kite-emulation (commit a165225: aerodynamic.billowing_percentage, aerodynamic.wing_load_scale) and kept out of develop as empirical tuning. That reading was wrong on both counts. The 5 % is measured (Haanen) and reproduced by Billow full. The 12.9 % is the TE-node area deficit of the PSM mesh. In the emulation gate, those options closed the tape mismatch with V3Kite but not the ~28 % wing-drag gap, so that gap has another cause.
