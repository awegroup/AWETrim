Rigid-body load transfer onto the struts (strut lever)#

How AWETrim moves the aerodynamic panel loads of VSM onto the nodes of a wireframe kite structure so that both the resultant force and the resultant moment are conserved exactly. This is the method of Cayon (2022, MSc thesis, Sec. 6.3), completed with the one term that thesis implementation dropped.

Code: awetrim.aerostructural.mapping.StrutLeverAeroToStructuralLoadMapper, selected with aerodynamic.load_transfer: strut_lever (default bilinear). Tests: tests/aerostructural/test_mapping.py.

1. Problem#

The aerodynamic model (VSM) discretises the wing in $N$ spanwise panels. Panel $i$ returns a force $\mathbf F_i$ and a moment. The structural model (the wireframe) has far fewer nodes: one rib per inflatable strut, each rib with a front node $\mathbf f$ and a rear node $\mathbf r$ (on the reduced LEI-V3 wireframe, the front and rear bridle attachments at $x/c \approx 0.01$ and $0.875$). Each panel lies in one bay, between the struts $A$ and $B$ of two consecutive ribs, and its load must be replaced by four nodal forces $\mathbf q_{fA}, \mathbf q_{rA}, \mathbf q_{fB}, \mathbf q_{rB}$ on those ribs.

The structure only feels the nodal forces, so the transfer must be statically equivalent: about any point $\mathbf o$,

$$ \sum_n \mathbf q_n = \mathbf F_i, \qquad \sum_n (\mathbf x_n - \mathbf o)\times\mathbf q_n = (\mathbf x_i - \mathbf o)\times\mathbf F_i + \mathbf m_i , $$

where $\mathbf x_i$ is the point the panel force acts at and $\mathbf m_i$ the panel’s own moment about it. Six equations, twelve unknowns: the transfer is a choice of one statically equivalent distribution. Force conservation alone is easy; it is the moment that sets the pitch balance of every bay, and with it the strut twist and the depower response of the kite.

Panel load as AWETrim defines it#

VSM gives each panel’s force $\mathbf F_i$, its moment $\mathbf M_i$ about the solver’s reference point $\mathbf r_\mathrm{ref}$, and a per-panel centre of pressure $\mathbf c_i$, the point on the chord where $\mathbf F_i$ alone would reproduce the panel’s pitching moment. VSM clamps the lever of that point to $[-0.25c, 0.75c]$, so $\mathbf F_i$ at $\mathbf c_i$ is not always the whole load. The transfer therefore takes

$$ \mathbf x_i = \mathbf c_i, \qquad \mathbf m_i = \mathbf M_i - (\mathbf c_i - \mathbf r_\mathrm{ref})\times\mathbf F_i , $$

which is exact for any choice of $\mathbf x_i$: $\mathbf m_i$ is whatever moment the force at $\mathbf x_i$ does not already carry (zero where VSM’s centre of pressure is unclamped).

2. The thesis method (Cayon 2022, Sec. 6.3)#

The thesis treats the region between the aerodynamic and the structural mesh as a rigid body and moves each panel load by lever rules (functions_PSM.force2nodes, moment2nodes):

  1. Chordwise. The line through $\mathbf x_i$ along the local chord $\hat{\mathbf t}$ meets the front line $\overline{\mathbf f_A\mathbf f_B}$ at $\mathbf P_1$ and the rear line $\overline{\mathbf r_A\mathbf r_B}$ at $\mathbf P_2$. The force is split between them by the lever rule: $\mathbf F_{P_2} = \mathbf M_1\times\mathbf d/|\mathbf d|^2$ with $\mathbf M_1 = (\mathbf x_i - \mathbf P_1)\times\mathbf F_i$ and $\mathbf d = \mathbf P_2 - \mathbf P_1$; $\mathbf F_{P_1} = \mathbf F_i - \mathbf F_{P_2}$.

  2. Spanwise. Each of $\mathbf F_{P_1}$, $\mathbf F_{P_2}$ is split between the two nodes of its line by the same lever rule.

  3. Section moment. The pitching moment is converted into a couple of two opposite forces a short distance apart along the chord, and each is moved by steps 1-2.

Every step conserves force. Step 2 also conserves moment exactly, because $\mathbf P_1$ lies on its line. Step 1 conserves moment only if the chord line through $\mathbf x_i$ really meets both lines: then $\mathbf M_1 \perp \mathbf d$ and $\mathbf d\times\mathbf F_{P_2} = \mathbf M_1$. That holds for a flat plate with the force point in its plane and the nodes at the leading and trailing edge, which was the thesis geometry.

line_intersect returns the closest point of the front (rear) line to the chord line, not an intersection. When the lines are skew, step 1 silently drops the component of $\mathbf M_1$ along $\mathbf d$ and the moment of the offset between the chord line and the closest point. On the reduced LEI-V3 wireframe the chord lines miss the rib lines by up to 241 mm (mean 73 mm unbillowed, 92 mm billowed), because

  • the rib nodes are bridle attachments, not the leading and trailing edge;

  • each bay is warped by the anhedral: its front and rear lines are skew;

  • the billowed panels lie off the plane of their bay.

The result is a 2-5 % loss of the wing’s pitching moment (Sec. 5). The thesis code checked only the force (the moment check is commented out in get_aero_forces2nodes), so the loss was not visible.

3. Strut lever: the exact rigid-body transfer#

The same rigid-body idea, with the moment of every move carried along instead of dropped. A strut is the straight line from its front to its rear node, $\mathbf d_A = \mathbf r_A - \mathbf f_A$, $\mathbf d_B = \mathbf r_B - \mathbf f_B$.

Step 1 - lever rule along the span. Take the closest points of the two strut lines to $\mathbf x_i$,

$$ t_k = \frac{(\mathbf x_i - \mathbf f_k)\cdot\mathbf d_k}{\mathbf d_k\cdot\mathbf d_k},\qquad \mathbf p_k = \mathbf f_k + t_k,\mathbf d_k ,\qquad k \in {A, B}, $$

and split the force between them by the position of $\mathbf x_i$ along $\mathbf s = \mathbf p_B - \mathbf p_A$:

$$ \sigma = \frac{(\mathbf x_i - \mathbf p_A)\cdot\mathbf s}{\mathbf s\cdot\mathbf s},\qquad \mathbf F_A = (1-\sigma),\mathbf F_i ,\qquad \mathbf F_B = \sigma,\mathbf F_i . $$

Force is conserved: $\mathbf F_A + \mathbf F_B = \mathbf F_i$.

Step 2 - the moment the move costs. About $\mathbf x_i$, the moved forces carry $(\mathbf p_A - \mathbf x_i)\times\mathbf F_A + (\mathbf p_B - \mathbf x_i)\times\mathbf F_B$. The struts still owe

$$ \mathbf R = \mathbf m_i - (\mathbf p_A - \mathbf x_i)\times\mathbf F_A - (\mathbf p_B - \mathbf x_i)\times\mathbf F_B . $$

When $\mathbf x_i$ lies on the segment $\mathbf p_A\mathbf p_B$ (a flat bay) the bracket vanishes and $\mathbf R = \mathbf m_i$. Otherwise $\mathbf R$ is exactly the term the thesis implementation dropped.

Step 3 - the owed moment onto the struts. A strut, two nodes on one line, can carry a moment about any axis normal to itself, as a front/rear force pair, but none about its own axis. The part of $\mathbf R$ about a strut axis (a twist about the chord) goes across the span as a couple: $+\mathbf G$ on strut $B$, $-\mathbf G$ on strut $A$. So

$$ \mathbf R = \mathbf M_A + \mathbf M_B + \mathbf s\times\mathbf G , \qquad \mathbf M_A\perp\mathbf d_A,\quad \mathbf M_B\perp\mathbf d_B,\quad \mathbf G\perp\mathbf s . $$

Each unknown has two components (six in all) for three equations. AWETrim takes the minimum-norm solution, with $\mathbf G$ scaled by the span $|\mathbf s|$ so that every unknown is a moment of comparable size. The system is singular only if a strut runs along the span, which an LEI wing does not do.

Step 4 - each strut onto its two nodes. The force on strut $k$, $\mathbf F_k \mp \mathbf G$ at $\mathbf p_k$, splits by the lever rule along the strut; the couple $\mathbf M_k$ becomes equal and opposite node forces:

$$ \mathbf q_{fk} = (1 - t_k)(\mathbf F_k \mp \mathbf G) - \frac{\mathbf M_k\times\mathbf d_k}{|\mathbf d_k|^2},\qquad \mathbf q_{rk} = t_k(\mathbf F_k \mp \mathbf G) + \frac{\mathbf M_k\times\mathbf d_k}{|\mathbf d_k|^2}, $$

with the upper sign for $A$. The lever split has zero moment about $\mathbf p_k$ because $\mathbf p_k$ is on the strut, and the couple’s moment is $\mathbf d_k\times(\mathbf M_k\times\mathbf d_k)/|\mathbf d_k|^2 = \mathbf M_k$ because $\mathbf M_k\perp\mathbf d_k$.

Exactness. Steps 1, 2 and 4 are identities in force and moment, and step 3 solves for the remainder exactly, so the four nodal forces reproduce the panel force and its moment about any point, on warped bays and for points off the bay.

Reduction to the thesis. On a flat rectangle with $\mathbf x_i$ inside it, $\mathbf p_A$, $\mathbf p_B$ and $\mathbf x_i$ are collinear, $\mathbf R = \mathbf m_i$ and the split is the lever rule along the chord and along the span: the bilinear split, and the thesis’.

Which bay. Each panel is assigned to its bay by construction (the aerodynamic mesh is built section by section between the struts, billowing.panel_bays), not by sorting on the span coordinate $y$. At the curled LEI-V3 tip $y$ is not monotonic, and a $y$ search gave the tip panels a degenerate bay (one front node twice), which the bilinear mapper accepted silently and on which the thesis code divides by zero.

4. Relation to other exact choices#

Any statically equivalent distribution is exact; they differ only in how the load is shared among the four nodes (the six-dimensional null space). The plain minimum-norm solution of the six equations is also exact and differs from the strut lever by less than 10 N per node on the LEI-V3 wing at $\alpha = 8°$ (of up to 400 N). The strut lever is kept because it is the thesis’ lever-rule physics wherever the geometry allows it, and solves only for the remainder.

5. Verification#

Synthetic (tests/aerostructural/test_mapping.py): 2000 random warped bays with points off the bay and random forces and moments conserve force and moment to $10^{-13}$; a flat rectangle reproduces the bilinear split; a pure twist about the chord goes across the span as a couple; zero load gives zero; mirrored bay and load give mirrored forces.
