Newton or Lagrange for the quasi-steady trim: why the Lagrangian form is not faster#

Question (2026-10-10). The reduced-order model writes the kite’s dynamics as a force balance in the course-aligned frame (SystemModel.force_residual, -m a + F_ext, Cayon et al. 2026 WES). Would deriving the same model from a Lagrangian make the trajectory NLP cheaper?

Answer. No. The Lagrangian derivation yields the same three equations, the inertia terms are 0.3 % of each node function, and function evaluations are a quarter of IPOPT’s wall time. The numbers below come from compare_lagrangian_residual.py (available on request) (LEI-V3, periodic B-spline reel-out, M = 10, flight-corrected aerostructural ROM). A typeset twin is lagrangian_formulation.pdf (rebuild with latexmk -pdf lagrangian_formulation.tex in this folder).

The two derivations#

The path is prescribed on the unit sphere, q(s) = (phi(s), beta(s)), and the kite sits at R = r q(s). The course frame has x along the path tangent, z radially outward and y normal to the path on the sphere.

Newton (library). Velocity v = (v_tau, 0, v_r) with v_tau = r sigma' s_dot, sigma' = |q'|, and the frame rotation rate omega = (0, v_tau / r, (v_tau / r) tan(beta) sin(chi) - chi_dot). The acceleration is a = (v_tau_dot, 0, v_r_dot) + omega x v; quasi-steady drops the first bracket. Three rows: F_ext - m a = 0.

Lagrange. Generalised coordinates (s, r), the path being a holonomic constraint. Kinetic energy T = m/2 (r_dot^2 + r^2 sigma'^2 s_dot^2), generalised forces Q_s = F . dR/ds = r sigma' F_x and Q_r = F_z. The Euler-Lagrange equations are

s:  d/dt(m r^2 sigma'^2 s_dot) - m r^2 sigma' sigma'' s_dot^2 = r sigma' F_x
r:  m (r_ddot - r sigma'^2 s_dot^2)                           = F_z

Expanding the s row gives m r sigma' (v_tau_dot + v_tau v_r / r), so the quasi-steady assumption v_tau_dot = 0 is a condition on s_ddot, not s_ddot = 0. The lateral force the steering has to supply is the constraint multiplier of the path, m k_g v_tau^2 / r with k_g the geodesic curvature. Divided by the metric factors, the three rows are

F_x - m v_tau v_r / r = 0
F_y + m k_g v_tau^2 / r = 0
F_z + m v_tau^2 / r = 0

which are the Newton rows. Over 200 random states the two residuals agree to 3.5e-6 relative; the difference is the fmax floor on v_tau and the 1e-6 guard in tan(beta) of the library form. The multiplier row cannot be dropped, since it is the equation that determines u_s, so no equation and no unknown is eliminated.

The whole acceleration costs 8877 instructions, but 26 of them are the inertia; the rest is the spline evaluation and its s-derivatives, which the aerodynamic force needs as well (the apparent wind is built in the course frame). The Lagrangian residual, written through curvature_geodesic, is twice as large because the derivative chain of the spline is built a second time. The Jacobians scale the same way (13460 against 22074).

A reformulation of the inertia terms can therefore change at most a millisecond per solve. The levers that remain are the spline-kinematics graph in ParametrizedKinematics (nested ca.gradient chains, ~90 % of the node operations), the iteration count, and the KKT size.

Follow-up: the remaining levers, measured#

Same 120-node reel-out NLP, single runs, so differences of a few milliseconds per iteration are noise.

Spline-kinematics graph. The properties of ParametrizedKinematics rebuild the spline sub-graph on every access (phi alone is evaluated about nine times inside one node function) and the SX expansion keeps the copies. Written with analytic B-spline derivatives, the course-frame chain chi, v_tau, chi_dot, k_g costs 470 instructions instead of 4951 (agreement 1e-11). Yet the solve does not get faster: 56 iterations either way, 24 vs 26 ms per iteration without turn-radius rows, 31 vs 28 ms with them (R_min 25 m, 95 vs 94 iterations). Function evaluations stay at 25 % of the wall time; the rest is the KKT back-solve, which L-BFGS turns into many right-hand sides per iteration. The smaller graph still cuts the whole-graph expansion memory in proportion, which matters at the full-cycle size (3.7 MB per node at N = 376), and would be a safe change: the mathematics is unchanged.

Hessian. The exact Hessian (available from the SX graph) halves the iterations, 26 instead of 56, but the Hessian evaluation costs 0.69 s and the solve ends at 1.66 s against 1.36 s. With the turn-radius rows it is far worse: 245 iterations and 29 s, the sigma'^3 polynomial rows giving an ill-conditioned Hessian. A 20-deep L-BFGS memory changes nothing (57 and 84 iterations). The production choice, L-BFGS with the default memory, stands.

Conclusion. At this problem size the NLP is already balanced between function evaluation and linear algebra, and no single lever gains more than the 25 % that function evaluation represents. Wall time is governed by the iteration count and the number of staged passes, not by the cost of the model.
