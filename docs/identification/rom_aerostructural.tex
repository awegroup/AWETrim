\begin{abstract}
\noindent
The quasi-steady reduced-order model (ROM) of Cayon, van Deursen and
Schmehl~\cite{cayon2026rom} needs a lift and a drag polar, the bridle pitch
$\theta_b$ between the bridle resultant and the wing chord, and a steering gain.
This note identifies \emph{every} one of them from coupled vortex-step-method
(VSM) and Billow-wireframe solutions at the centre of the wind window. Near
stall the coupled problem has more than one solution, and which one a
continuation finds depends on the way it was reached: marching the angle of
attack up stays attached far past the stall of the same wing marched down. The
state marched down is the one that agrees with reality, so every state of the
data set is moved to it and every polar is the march down. The lift and drag
polynomials are selected by forward--backward stepwise regression on a
cross-validation error that holds out whole frozen shapes, and include a smooth
stall switch. The range of the controls the ROM may be flown in follows from
the same data: a depower setting is valid if a gust does not stall it.
\end{abstract}

\section{Purpose and scope}

The ROM is a point mass in the course reference frame whose wing is assumed to
align instantaneously with the bridle resultant~\cite{cayon2026rom}. Its
aerodynamic inputs are
\begin{itemize}
  \item the wing lift and drag coefficients $C_L$, $C_D$, as functions of the
        wing angle of attack $\alpha_w$ and the controls;
  \item the geometric bridle pitch $\theta_b$ that turns the bridle angle of
        attack $\alpha_b$ into the wing angle of attack,
        $\alpha_w=\alpha_b-\theta_b$ (paper Eqs.~1--2);
  \item the steering gain $k_{\phi,s}$ of the control-induced aerodynamic roll,
        $\phi_{a,w}=k_{\phi,s}\,u_s$ (paper Eq.~43), which depends on the depower and
        on the steering itself.
\end{itemize}
The ROM (\code{rom\_config\_aerostructural.yaml}) is identified from simulations
only: the VSM aerodynamics of the \emph{deformed} kite, coupled to the Billow
structural model.

The note is written so that the identification can be repeated with another
aerostructural tool: Sect.~\ref{sec:model} states the model,
Sect.~\ref{sec:geometry} defines every angle as a formula on vectors any tool
provides, Sect.~\ref{sec:data} builds the data set, Sect.~\ref{sec:method} fits the
ROM parameters to it and Sect.~\ref{sec:limits} sets the limits of the controls.

Scope and limits, stated once:
\begin{itemize}
  \item centre of the wind window (azimuth and elevation $0$), no reel speed,
        steady flight, gravity off (the ROM models gravity itself);
  \item the stall comes from VSM with artificial viscosity on frozen shapes;
  \item the coefficients describe the wing and its bridle lines: the ROM adds the
        KCU drag and the tether itself.
\end{itemize}

\section{The model}
\label{sec:model}

\subsection{Inputs and conventions}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $u_p$ is the power-tape length $l_\mathrm{dp}$ in metres.
  \item $u_s$ is the \emph{standardised} steering of
        \code{awetrim.identification.controls}: $\SI{1.4}{\metre}\times u_s$ is the
        steering-tape half-difference (2019 KCU: $u_s=-\mathrm{kcu}/100$, full
        deflection $\pm0.35$); $u_s>0$ turns the kite to its right.
  \item $\alpha\equiv\alpha_w$ is the angle of attack of the wing's
        centre-panel chord (Sect.~\ref{sec:geometry}).
  \item $v_a$ is the apparent wind speed.
  \item All coefficients are referenced to $S=\SI{\refArea}{\square\metre}$, the
        EKF/flight area; the ROM file states
        it (\code{aerodynamics.reference\_area}). It is \emph{not} the projected
        area of the geometry (\SI{17.21}{\square\metre}).
\end{itemize}
A tool with another actuation model replaces $u_p$ and $u_s$ by its own
inputs; what has to be identified against them is the same.

\subsection{Lift and drag}
Both coefficients are linear combinations of plain monomials,
\begin{equation}
  C_L = c_0 + \sum_i c_i\, m_i(\alpha,u_p,u_s,v_a,\sigma), \qquad
  C_D = d_0 + \sum_j d_j\, m_j(\alpha,u_p,u_s,v_a,\sigma),
  \label{eq:polys}
\end{equation}
where $\sigma(\alpha)$ is a smooth attached-to-separated switch,
\begin{equation}
  \sigma(\alpha) = \tfrac12\left[1+\tanh\!\left(\frac{\alpha-\alpha_s}{w}\right)\right],
  \label{eq:stall}
\end{equation}
with stall angle $\alpha_s$ and width $w$ (ROM parameters
\code{angle\_of\_attack\_stall}, \code{width\_stall}). Terms multiplied by
$\sigma$ act only past the stall, so $C=C_\mathrm{att}+\sigma\,(C_\mathrm{sep}-C_\mathrm{att})$
stays linear in its coefficients. Every term is smooth: the ROM evaluates
monomials as written (a term may ask for $|m|$ with \code{abs: true}; the
identification never does).

\subsection{Bridle pitch and steering}
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_w=\alpha_b-\theta_b,\qquad
  \theta_b=\theta_0+\theta_1\,u_p,\qquad
  \phi_{a,w}=k_{\phi,s}(u_p,u_s)\,u_s ,
\end{equation}
with $\theta_0$, $\theta_1$, $k_{\phi,s}$ the ROM parameters
\code{angle\_pitch\_tether\_0}, \code{slope\_angle\_pitch\_tether\_depower},
\code{gain\_roll\_steering}.

\subsection{KCU drag, a separate force}
$C_D$ is the \emph{wing's} drag, bridle lines included. The kite control unit
is a separate bluff body hanging on the tether at the bridle point (ROM flag
\code{kcu\_drag\_in\_coefficients: false}), with the finite-cylinder law of
\code{awetrim.aerodynamics.kcu\_drag},
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf F_\mathrm{KCU}=\tfrac12\rho\left(A_\parallel\,|\mathbf v_\parallel|\,\mathbf v_\parallel
  + A_\perp\,|\mathbf v_\perp|\,\mathbf v_\perp\right),
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf v_\parallel$, $\mathbf v_\perp$ split the apparent wind about the
tether, and the drag areas come from the system file's KCU length, diameter and
onboard turbine. The KCU drag enters the total aerodynamic force \emph{and} the
bridle resultant $\mathbf F_b$ (tether plus the KCU's weight, inertia and drag),
so swapping the KCU changes $\mathbf F_b$ without re-identifying the wing.

\section{Geometric definitions}
\label{sec:geometry}
Every angle the ROM needs is stated here as a formula on vectors, so that the
parameters can be extracted from any tool's output without AWETrim.
Fig.~\ref{fig:geometry} draws them. Vectors are in any common Cartesian frame.

\paragraph{The two angles of attack.} They have different roles:
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\alpha_w$, the angle of the apparent wind to the \emph{centre-panel
        chord}, is the independent variable of the polars. Every $C_L$, $C_D$
        sample, from a trim or from an angle-of-attack sweep, is recorded
        against $\alpha_w$, and the ROM evaluates its polynomials at
        $\alpha_w$.
  \item $\alpha_b$, the angle of the apparent wind to the \emph{bridle
        resultant} $\mathbf F_b$, is what the point-mass ROM computes from its
        own forces. It serves one purpose: the bridle pitch
        $\theta_b=\alpha_b-\alpha_w$, the angle between the chord and the
        bridle resultant at trim, set by the bridle geometry and the depower
        setting, through which the ROM recovers $\alpha_w=\alpha_b-\theta_b$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Symbols.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $\mathbf v_a$: the apparent wind at the wing, the velocity of the air
        relative to the kite (it flows from leading to trailing edge);
        $\hat{\mathbf v}_a=\mathbf v_a/|\mathbf v_a|$.
  \item $\mathbf e_r$: the unit radial direction, ground station to kite.
  \item $\mathbf F_w$: the aerodynamic force on the wing \emph{and its bridle
        lines}, without the KCU and the tether.
  \item $\mathbf F_b$: the bridle resultant, the force the bridle lines carry
        to the wing from the bridle point $B$. It is the tether force at $B$
        plus the KCU's weight, inertia ($-m_\mathrm{KCU}\mathbf a$) and drag;
        equivalently, from the wing's own balance with wing mass $m_w$,
        $\mathbf F_b=-\bigl(\mathbf F_w+m_w(\mathbf g-\mathbf a)\bigr)$, which
        for a gravity-off trim without acceleration is $-\mathbf F_w$.
  \item $P_\mathrm{LE}$, $P_\mathrm{TE}$: the mid-span leading- and
        trailing-edge points of the \emph{centre panel} (the panel spanning
        the centreline, between the two innermost struts) of the
        \emph{deformed} wing; $\mathbf s$ that panel's span direction, from
        the left to the right strut.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Wing axes.} Aircraft FRD axes anchored to the centre panel:
\begin{align}
  \mathbf x_K&=\frac{P_\mathrm{LE}-P_\mathrm{TE}}{|P_\mathrm{LE}-P_\mathrm{TE}|} && \text{chord, forward},\nonumber\\
  \mathbf z_K&=\frac{\mathbf x_K\times\mathbf s}{|\mathbf x_K\times\mathbf s|} && \text{down, toward the bridle},\\
  \mathbf y_K&=\mathbf z_K\times\mathbf x_K && \text{right wing}.\nonumber
\end{align}

\paragraph{Wing angle of attack.} With $\mathbf u=-\mathbf v_a$ the kite's
velocity through the air,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_w=\operatorname{atan2}\bigl(\mathbf u\cdot\mathbf z_K,\ \mathbf u\cdot\mathbf x_K\bigr),
  \label{eq:alpha-w}
\end{equation}
positive nose up.

\paragraph{Lift and drag coefficients.}
$D=\mathbf F_w\cdot\hat{\mathbf v}_a$, $L=\bigl|\mathbf F_w-D\,\hat{\mathbf v}_a\bigr|$
(positive on the $-\mathbf z_K$ side), $C_{L,D}=\{L,D\}/(\tfrac12\rho v_a^2S)$
with $S=\SI{\refArea}{\square\metre}$.

\paragraph{The apparent-wind frame.} The ROM takes the bridle angles in the
frame of paper App.~D, built from $\mathbf v_a$ and $\mathbf e_r$ only:
\begin{equation}
  \mathbf e_{r'}=\frac{\mathbf e_r-(\mathbf e_r\cdot\hat{\mathbf v}_a)\hat{\mathbf v}_a}{|\cdot|}
  \ \text{(zero-roll lift direction)},\qquad
  \mathbf e_{p}=\frac{\mathbf v_a\times\mathbf e_r}{|\mathbf v_a\times\mathbf e_r|}
  \ \text{(toward the left wing)}.
\end{equation}
($\mathbf e_p=-\mathbf e_{n'}$ of paper Eq.~D6; in a straight flight
$\mathbf e_p\approx-\mathbf y_K$.)

\paragraph{Bridle angle of attack and bridle pitch.} With
$\mathbf P=-\mathbf F_b$, the pull of the wing on the bridle,
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_b=\operatorname{atan2}\bigl(\mathbf P\cdot\hat{\mathbf v}_a,\ \mathbf P\cdot\mathbf e_{r'}\bigr)
  \quad\text{(paper Eq.~D11)},\qquad
  \theta_b=\alpha_b-\alpha_w .
  \label{eq:alpha-b}
\end{equation}
$\alpha_b$ is the angle by which the pull leans downstream from
$\mathbf e_{r'}$; for a massless kite without KCU drag it is the glide angle,
$\tan\alpha_b=C_D/C_L$, a useful check. In a symmetric trim ($\mathbf v_a$,
$\mathbf e_r$ and $\mathbf F_b$ in the wing's symmetry plane) the bridle
pitch follows from the trimmed geometry alone, independent of the inflow:
\begin{equation}
  \sin\theta_b=\mathbf x_K\cdot\hat{\mathbf F}_b ,
\end{equation}
the angle by which the centre chord's leading edge dips toward the bridle
resultant from the perpendicular. $\theta_b>0$ (the LEI-V3 values) means the
nose is pitched down relative to that perpendicular, so $\alpha_w<\alpha_b$.
In the paper's terms $\theta_b=\theta_d+\lambda_b$ (Eq.~2); the ROM does not
split it, it fits $\theta_b(u_p)$ directly. $\theta_b$ is always taken
against the bridle resultant, never against the tether direction: the two
differ by the KCU loads.

\paragraph{Roll angles.} The roll of a force $\mathbf F$ about
$\mathbf v_a$, zero along $\mathbf e_{r'}$ and positive toward the left wing,
is
\begin{equation}
  \phi(\mathbf F)=\operatorname{atan2}\bigl(\mathbf F\cdot\mathbf e_p,\ \mathbf F\cdot\mathbf e_{r'}\bigr).
  \label{eq:roll}
\end{equation}
The bridle roll is $\phi_{a,b}=\phi(\mathbf P)$, the total aerodynamic roll
$\phi_a=\phi(\mathbf L)$ with $\mathbf L=\mathbf F_w-D\,\hat{\mathbf v}_a$,
and the steering roll is their difference,
$\phi_{a,w}=\phi_a-\phi_{a,b}=k_{\phi,s}u_s$ (paper Eqs.~22, D13--D14). A
positive $u_s$ turns the kite to the right as seen from the kite; with
$k_{\phi,s}<0$ it tilts the lift toward the right wing ($\phi_{a,w}<0$) and
the course rate is $\dot\chi>0$. Note for readers of the paper: App.~D1
describes a positive $\phi_a$ as a right-hand turn, but its Eqs.~D6--D9 (and
the code) tilt the lift toward the left wing for $\phi_a>0$; this note
follows the equations. Main-text Eq.~24 also has the opposite sign of the
$\sin\phi_a$ terms to Eq.~D9; the code uses Eq.~D9.

\section{The data set}
\label{sec:data}
Every state of the data set is reached along a chain, solved by the coupled model,
moved to the realistic stall branch, and then given a polar. Two kinds of data
come out, and the second is not optional: converged trims, and angle-of-attack
sweeps on their frozen shapes.

\subsection{The chains}
\label{sec:data-trims}
All states are at the centre of the wind window: course \SI{90}{\degree}, no reel
speed, steady flight, uniform wind, gravity off. A chain is a sequence of states,
each starting from the one before. A depower chain ends when its stalled span
fraction exceeds \num{0.2}, a steering chain at the end of its range, and either at a
state that does not converge.
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Depower chains}, at zero steering, walk the power tape from the
        length the geometry is built at past both ends of the band, at several wind
        speeds, reaching the apparent wind speeds of reel-out and reel-in. They give
        $\theta_b(u_p)$ and the depower and load terms of the polars.
  \item \textbf{Steering chains} walk the power tape to a set length, then grow the
        steering half-difference (one side suffices; left and right are mirror
        images), at lengths across the band including its powered end. They give
        $k_{\phi,s}(u_p)$, the steering terms and the steering limit.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{The coupled solve}
The kite's structural file is reduced to the wireframe (cables, tension-only lines,
pulleys and the wing's struts), and the wing is meshed with \num{135} VSM panels on
the billowed geometry, the loads transferred by the strut-lever rule. At each state
the coupled loop moves the tapes, solves the structure by energy minimisation, trims
the kite with a Newton solve on the trim states and the circulation, transfers the
loads and repeats until the forces settle. The trim balances the forces and the
moments about the bridle point, with the tether force at that point, and solves for
the flight speed and, for a steered state, the course rate. A Newton solve that does
not reach its tolerance reports the lowest residual it visited, and is accepted only
near a root.

\subsection{The stall branch}
\label{sec:branches}
A wing past its stall has more than one solution for the circulation, and a seeded
solve stays on the one it starts near. The chains approach the powered tapes from
the attached side, so they stay attached past the point at which the same wing,
marched down in angle of attack from a fully stalled state, is already separated
(Fig.~\ref{fig:branches}). The march down is the stall that agrees with reality, and
it is the one identified:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item from the converged state, freeze the shape and march the angle of attack up
        past the stall and back down to the trim's own inflow, keeping that
        circulation;
  \item solve the coupled problem again from it, structure and trim free.
\end{enumerate}
A state with one solution comes back to itself. A state with two stays on the lower
one, at a higher angle of attack and a lower tension, and that re-solved state is
the data point. Step~2 is the test: the frozen march alone does not say that the kite
stays there, and the coupled solve is free to go back. The maximum of the march must
be well past the stall, or the march down has not fully separated and its stall
reads too high.

\subsection{The polars}
\label{sec:data-sweeps}
Trims alone cannot identify the polars. Along a depower chain $\alpha_w$ follows
$u_p$ almost one to one, so a fit on trims cannot tell the polar slope from the
depower effect, while in flight gravity and turning move $\alpha_w$ by several
degrees around the trim value. Around every state:
\begin{enumerate}
  \item freeze the converged deformed shape, and keep $|\mathbf v_a|$, the sideslip
        and the body rates of the trim;
  \item solve the aerodynamics at $\Delta\alpha_w=0$ from the trim's own circulation,
        which returns the trim's force;
  \item rotate the inflow about $\mathbf y_K$ in steps of \SI{1}{\degree}, up to
        \SI{+22}{\degree} about the trim, and then back down to \SI{-6}{\degree}, each
        solve seeded with the one before and without re-trimming (the moment about
        the bridle point is not balanced; this is the polar of that shape);
  \item record $\alpha_w$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:alpha-w}), $\mathbf v_a$, $\mathbf F_w$ and,
        separately, $\mathbf F_\mathrm{KCU}$ at each step of the march \emph{down}.
\end{enumerate}
The $\Delta\alpha_w=0$ step must reproduce the trim's force within \SI{0.1}{\percent},
or the sweep is discarded. Solves that do not converge, which happen above the stall,
are dropped from the fit.

\section{The fit}
\label{sec:method}
The samples are the converged states inside the fit band of the power tape
(Sect.~\ref{sec:limits}), and the polars that pass the check of
Sect.~\ref{sec:data-sweeps}.

\subsection{Bridle pitch}
$\theta_b=\alpha_b-\alpha_w$ (Eq.~\ref{eq:alpha-b}) is fitted linearly in $u_p$
by ordinary least squares on the unsteered trims. With gravity off the coupled
equilibrium is $\mathbf F_w+\mathbf F_\mathrm{KCU}+\mathbf F_t+\mathbf F_\mathrm{in}=0$,
with $\mathbf F_\mathrm{in}$ the inertial force, and the bridle resultant is
$\mathbf F_b=-(\mathbf F_w+\tfrac{m_w}{m}\mathbf F_\mathrm{in})$.

\subsection{Steering gain}
$\phi_{a,w}$ per $u_s$ (Sect.~\ref{sec:geometry}) is fitted as a polynomial in the
depower plus an even term in the steering,
$k_{\phi,s}(u_p,u_s)=k_0+k_1u_p+k_2u_p^2+(s_0+s_1u_p)\,u_s^2$, with both degrees chosen by leave-one-chain-out
cross-validation, on the steered trims with a stalled span fraction up to \num{0.2}.

\subsection{Stall location}
$\alpha_s$ is measured, not fitted: it is the median stall onset
(Sect.~\ref{sec:limits}) of the unsteered states near their stall, $u_p=1.40$ to
\SI{1.70}{\metre} for the LEI-V3, which gives \SI{10.27}{\degree}, with
$w=\SI{1}{\degree}$. Chosen on the fit instead, it would follow the many samples of the
plateau rather than the few at the lift drop. The coefficients are then fitted on
the samples up to \SI{8}{\degree} past $\alpha_s$, on states with a stalled span
fraction up to \num{0.2}. The ROM is not flown past the stall, so its operating range
is what the fit serves; past $\alpha_s$ it follows the trend of the data, not the lift
drop itself.

\subsection{Candidate terms}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{lll}
\toprule
Group & Terms & Reason \\
\midrule
attached polar & $\alpha$, $\alpha^2$ & lift slope and curvature \\
depower & $u_p$, $u_p^2$, $\alpha u_p$, $\alpha^2u_p$ & shape change with the power tape \\
steering & $u_s^2$, $\alpha u_s^2$, $u_pu_s^2$ & symmetric in left/right steering \\
load & $v_a$, $\alpha v_a$, $u_pv_a$ & deformation grows with dynamic pressure \\
stall & $\sigma$, $\sigma\alpha$, $\sigma\alpha^2$, $\sigma u_p$, $\sigma u_s^2$ & active past the stall only \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}
There is no $\alpha^3$: the ROM's solvers do not bound $\alpha$, a cubic $C_D$
went negative beyond the data, and a cubic $C_L$ cannot follow the sharp stall.

\subsection{Least squares and selection}
For a term set $\mathcal T$, the coefficients minimise the sum of squared
residuals, $\hat{\mathbf c}=\arg\min_{\mathbf c}\lVert\mathbf y-X_\mathcal T\mathbf c\rVert_2^2$,
solved with each column scaled to unit RMS for conditioning and mapped back.

Polar samples of one frozen shape are strongly correlated, so the selection
score is the \emph{grouped} $K$-fold cross-validation error with whole anchors
held out ($K=5$):
\begin{equation}
  \mathrm{CV}(\mathcal T)=\frac1K\sum_{k=1}^K
  \sqrt{\frac{1}{|G_k|}\sum_{i\in G_k}\bigl(y_i-\hat y_i^{(-k)}\bigr)^2},
\end{equation}
where $\hat y^{(-k)}$ is fitted without fold $G_k$. A random split would score
interpolation inside a polar rather than prediction at an unseen state. BIC
is not used: it treats the correlated samples as independent evidence and
keeps accepting terms worth a fraction of a percent.

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Forward}: from the intercept, add the candidate that lowers
        $\mathrm{CV}$ most; stop when the best improves it by less than
        1\,\%.
  \item \textbf{Backward}: drop any term whose removal costs less than the same
        1\,\%; a term picked early can become redundant later.
  \item \textbf{Extrapolation check}: $C_D$ should stay positive over
        $\pm\SI{30}{\degree}$ across the fitted $u_p$, $u_s$, $v_a$ ranges; the
        script warns if it does not.
\end{enumerate}

\section{The limits of the controls}
\label{sec:limits}
The ROM is valid in a box of the controls, written to its \code{validity} block, to
which the optimiser's bounds are clipped.

\paragraph{Depower.}
A depower setting that is itself stalled is no operating point, and one a gust can
stall is not a safe one. The powered end of the band is the shortest tape for which
a gust stays below the stall onset of the march down:
\begin{equation}
  \alpha_w+\arctan\frac{\Delta v}{v_a}<\alpha_\mathrm{onset},
\end{equation}
with $\Delta v=\SI{1}{\metre\per\second}$ and $\alpha_\mathrm{onset}$ the angle at
which $C_L$ falls \SI{5}{\percent} below the attached curve extrapolated from the
polar below the trim. For the LEI-V3 this gives \SI{1.50}{\metre}. The fit band reaches
further, to the shortest tape not itself stalled, \SI{1.40}{\metre}: no stalled depower
setting enters the fit, its polars still reach past the stall, and the edge of the
ROM's band is not an extrapolation. The depowered end is the end of the sweep,
\SI{2.60}{\metre}. Figures~\ref{fig:trim-depower} and~\ref{fig:gust-margin} show this
along the depower chains.

\label{fig:gust-margin}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Angle of attack.}
The optimiser's upper bound is the stall less a margin for the same gust, \SI{3}{\degree}
for the LEI-V3, so \SI{7.27}{\degree}: the ROM's \code{validity} states what may be flown,
not how far the data reach.

\paragraph{Steering.}
Steering stalls one wing tip, earlier the more powered the kite is (Fig.~\ref{fig:steering}).
Steering chains therefore do not stop on stall: they run to the end of their range or to a
state that does not converge, which is what shows the limit. At \SI{1.50}{\metre} they end
at a failed trim at \SI{0.45}{\metre}, with the tension falling in steps from about
\SI{0.10}{\metre}, while the longer tapes reach the flown range. The course rate keeps
rising almost to the end, so it does not define a limit. The limit itself is still open
(Sect.~\ref{sec:open}).

\begin{figure}[H]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=\textwidth]{steering.pdf}
\caption{Course rate and tension against steering along the steering chains, \SI{5}{\metre\per\second} wind.}
\label{fig:steering}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Tension.}
The force margin depends on the wind, so it is a tension floor for the optimiser
rather than a limit on the tape: the tether force must stay above twice the KCU
weight after a \SI{15}{\percent} lull in speed (\SI{602}{\newton} for the LEI-V3).

\paragraph{What sets the turn rate.}
In a steady turn the KCU's inertia is carried through $\mathbf F_b$, so the wing only
supplies its own centripetal force, $L\,\phi_{a,w}\approx m_w v_\tau\dot\chi$. The turn
rate of a given gain therefore goes with the inverse of the \emph{wing's} mass, not the
kite's. The simulations carry \SI{13.44}{\kilogram} of wing and bridle. The check has to
use that mass: with the \SI{16.78}{\kilogram} of the flown system file it reads a turn
rate \SI{20}{\percent} low that has nothing to do with the gain.

\paragraph{Why no drag is fitted.}
At equal lift, the EKF's drag of wing and bridle compares to the simulated drag as in
Table~\ref{tab:ekf-drag}: slightly below it in straight flight, and well above it in turns,
where the simulated drag falls with steering. A drag fit reproduces those numbers but does
not improve the solve, for two reasons. Drag in the wing's $C_D$ tilts the bridle resultant
and raises $\alpha_w$, by about $G\,\Delta C_D/C_L$ with
$G=1/(1-\partial\epsilon/\partial\alpha_w)$ and $\epsilon=\arctan(C_D/C_L)$, some
\SI{5}{\degree} at full steering, so the kite pulls harder in the turns instead of slowing.
At the bridle point it leaves the trim alone but costs too much tension: matching the steered
reel-out tension takes almost none of it. The steering drag is therefore a finding, not a
parameter. The EKF is also least reliable in turns, where its innovation statistics are worse
and the inertial force is of the order of the drag.

\paragraph{What it means for the trim.}
That relation also gives the response to the tape, $\mathrm d\alpha_w/\mathrm du_p\approx
G\,(\partial\epsilon/\partial u_p-\theta_1)$, which matches the coupled trims within
\SI{0.5}{\degree\per\metre} RMS while $\theta_1$ alone misses by \SI{8.6}{\degree\per\metre}: $G$ falls from about 1.1 at
\SI{1.50}{\metre} to 0.23 at \SI{2.60}{\metre}, because $\theta_b$ and the glide angle grow together on
long tapes. So a drag change $\Delta C_D$ equals a tape change $\Delta C_D/(C_L\theta_1)$, which
depends on the lift: 0.01 is worth \SI{0.04}{\metre} of tape at \SI{1.50}{\metre} and \SI{0.13}{\metre} at
\SI{2.60}{\metre}. One drag offset cannot stand in for a tape error across the band.

\section{Limitations and open points}
\label{sec:open}
\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{The steering limit is not implemented yet.} The ROM's \code{validity} has
        one box, $|u_s|\le0.357$, while at \SI{1.50}{\metre} the last steered state that trims
        is \SI{0.425}{\metre} ($|u_s|=0.30$, at \SI{45}{\percent} of the unsteered tension) at
        all three wind speeds, short of the \SI{0.49}{\metre} flown. A limit
        $u_{s,\mathrm{max}}(u_p)$ still has to be defined and added to the optimiser.
  \item \textbf{Past the stall the ROM is not valid.} Its lift does not reproduce the
        drop, so the bound on $\alpha_w$ must stay active.
  \item \textbf{Stall data.} Several polar steps do not converge right around the stall,
        so the drop itself is poorly sampled, and the onset is read on steps of
        \SI{1}{\degree}. The stall is that of VSM on frozen deformed shapes.
  \item \textbf{Tether in the sweep.} The sweep's 10-element Williams tether carries too little drag
        (Sect.~\ref{sec:coupled-check}), so its absolute speeds and tensions read high. Any
        Williams use needs at least 40 elements.
  \item \textbf{Wing mass.} The simulations carry \SI{13.44}{\kilogram} of wing and bridle
        and the flown system file \SI{16.78}{\kilogram}. Since the turn rate depends on it, this
        is open for how much of a flight gain is aerodynamic.
  \item \textbf{Centre of the window, gravity off.} Off-centre, without weight, the wing's flow
        changes mainly through sideslip. The 2019 kinematics give a sideslip of $\mathbf v_a$ of about
        \SI{12}{\degree} at the median, and on the frozen trimmed shapes \SI{10}{\degree} of sideslip costs
        11--\SI{20}{\percent} more $C_D$. The EKF's drag shows no such penalty in straight flight, so the
        wing probably sees far less, by yawing into the flow. This frozen estimate neither re-trims
        nor re-deforms; coupled solves off-centre are open. Position-dependent aerodynamics are
        otherwise outside the data. The gravity-driven turn of the point-mass ROM is about twice the
        flight's (Table~\ref{tab:turn-law}).
  \item \textbf{Reel-in and the wind scatter.} The errors vary widely between cycles, following
        the estimated wind, and the solve loses samples quickly as the reel-in tape grows:
        about a fifth of them on the training cycles at the selected tape.
  \item \textbf{Flight data.} The EKF reconstruction is a model output too: its forces
        depend on its tether model and its coefficients are instantaneous at states that
        are not equilibria.
\end{itemize}

        with \path{--rom <rom> --steering measured} on cycles 60--67, after step 3;
  \item \path{examples/5_flight_data/identify_rom_flight_correction.py}, which runs the
        validator on the training cycles and writes \code{flight\_match} and the ROM file;
  \item the re-solve check, the flight plots and the tables come from
        \path{validate_rom_aerostructural.py}, \path{plot_rom_flight_validation.py},
        \path{plot_rom_turn_rate_law.py} and \path{report_rom_aerostructural.py} (available on
        request), then \path{latexmk -pdf} in \path{docs/identification/}. The stall-branch
        figure of Sect.~\ref{sec:data} comes from a one-off march of one frozen shape.
\end{enumerate}
